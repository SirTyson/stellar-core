## Meta-Patterns

1. **SHA256 / Hashing Budget Ceiling**: The entire in-apply SHA256 budget for soroswap is ~4 ms per ledger (~0.67% of apply). Any hypothesis that targets only SHA256 recomputation or TTL key derivation is structurally capped below the 1% Low floor, let alone the 3% Medium floor. Do not write SHA256-only hypotheses for this objective.

2. **Signature Verification Scope**: Signature verification and contents-hash computation happen during tx-set preparation, not during `closeLedger` apply. These are explicitly excluded from the optimize-soroswap objective scope. Profile traces must show the target zone as a descendant of `applyLedger` before writing a hypothesis.

3. **Host-Object Visit vs. Storage Map**: The dominant Soroban storage cost is `VisitObject` budget charging (host object table indexed by 32-bit handle), not the `MeteredOrdMap` binary search over XDR ledger keys. Hypotheses that target the map-probe side will under-estimate the true hotspot and fail the severity floor.

4. **Test/SDK Paths vs. Production Paths**: Some XDR serialization and event-size encoding paths exist only in test, SDK-style, or preflight instrumentation code. Confirm that the target code is reachable during production `closeLedger` before attributing apply time to it.

5. **Apply-Path verifySig and BLAKE2 Share Is Below 1%**: The apply-path share of `verifySig` work is bounded by `processSignatures` + `checkAllTransactionSignatures` = ~46ms across the full 65-ledger soroswap run (~0.7ms/ledger = <0.2% of apply median). Even bypassing the cache probe entirely cannot reach the 1% Low floor. No narrower micro-optimization within the apply-path verifySig zone can be promoted. This ceiling also bounds the BLAKE2 cache-probe prelude inside each verifySig call: apply-path BLAKE2 cannot exceed ~10ms total, and the headline `BLAKE2::add` self-time is dominated by out-of-scope overlay flood/peer/tracker callsites. The cache is already sharded 16 ways (H021). H022 (BLAKE2 cache-key memoization on `TransactionFrame`) is also bounded here. Future verifySig or BLAKE2 sub-optimizations on the apply path must cite a specific callsite that falls outside this ceiling.

6. **In-Memory Bucket Scan Cost Is Index Walk, Not Hash**: `InMemoryBucketState::scan` self-time (~2.1µs mean) is dominated by `unordered_set` bucket traversal and XDR equality compare, not by SipHash computation or `gKeyMutex` acquisition. Eliminating hashing or locking returns a small fraction of scan cost and cannot reach Medium severity; genuine wins require restructuring the index data structure.

7. **Tracy ZoneScoped Overhead Inflates Crypto Primitive Costs**: Fine-grained crypto primitives (SHA256::add, BLAKE2::add, XDRShortHasher constructor) accumulate significant Tracy zone entry/exit overhead that does not exist in non-Tracy production builds. Always size proposed savings against non-Tracy benchmark numbers, not against Tracy self-times.

8. **FFI Bridge Per-Entry Overhead Is Bounded at ~50ms Total**: Bridge input overhead (~22ms across the trace) and C++-side output decode overhead (~28ms) together total ~50ms. The Rust-side encoding work is required by soroban-env metering and cannot be skipped; realistic savings from restructuring the bridge are a small fraction of the output-decode share.

9. **Soroswap Uses SOURCE_ACCOUNT Credentials — In-Host Sig Verify Never Called**: The soroswap benchmark generates all Soroban auth entries with `SOROBAN_CREDENTIALS_SOURCE_ACCOUNT`. For source-account credentials, `AccountAuthorizationTracker::authenticate` short-circuits before reaching `check_account_authentication` or `verify_sig_ed25519_internal`. Any hypothesis targeting the Soroban host's in-host ed25519/address-credential verification path has zero soroswap apply-time impact. This also bounds auth-tree fingerprint optimizations: the soroswap auth tree has one root + one SAC-transfer child per tx, so linear-scan comparison work is minimal regardless of fingerprinting.

10. **Apply-Path verifySig Micro-Optimizations Are Fully Exhausted**: Meta-Pattern 5 establishes that the entire apply-path verifySig surface is <0.2% of apply time. Every sub-operation within that surface — SipHash shard-index computation, identity hasher for the cache, atomic counter increments, double-mutex acquisition on hit vs miss, Rust dalek flag atomic load, BLAKE2 one-shot vs streaming, type-tag/length prelude checks — has now been individually evaluated and rejected at hypothesis stage. No further micro-optimization within the apply-path verifySig zone can reach even the 1% Low floor. Do not propose any additional verifySig sub-operation optimization for this objective.

11. **Out-of-Scope Callers Inflate Crypto Zone Totals**: Several crypto functions (SecretKey::sign, KeyUtils::toStrKey, hkdfExpand) appear to have significant Tracy self-time but are entirely outside applyLedger windows in the soroswap trace. Their self-time is from benchmark tx-construction or overlay peer-auth paths. Always verify that a target zone is a descendant of applyLedger before attributing its self-time to the apply critical path.

12. **Parallel Worker Aggregate Time Is Not Wall-Clock Apply Impact**: Soroban host function calls and in-host operations execute inside `applySorobanStageClustersInParallel` worker threads. Aggregate zone self-time across all threads can appear to clear the Medium threshold (3% of apply), but must be divided by `APPLY_LOAD_LEDGER_MAX_DEPENDENT_TX_CLUSTERS` (typically 8) to estimate wall-clock impact. A syscall contributing 175ms aggregate worker time represents only ~22ms wall-clock — about 0.5% of the ~218ms apply median. Always normalize by cluster count before assessing severity.

13. **Intra-Cluster Task Parallelism Introduces Overhead**: Parallelizing non-conflicting transactions within a Soroswap-dependent cluster requires synchronization, scheduling overhead, and memory/cache pressure that can negate or exceed the savings. A PoC producing benchmark regression (even consistently) is a real result; the optimization must clear the objective's performance gate in actual measurement, not just in theoretical analysis.

14. **Opt-In Invariants Are Dead Code in Benchmarks**: `EventsAreConsistentWithEntryDiffs`, `BucketListIsConsistentWithDatabase`, and similar opt-in invariants are not registered in the standard apply-load benchmark configuration. Any Rust bridge calls, crypto operations, or FFI hops inside these invariants have zero apply-time impact on the benchmark metric. Verify that the invariant is enabled in the benchmark config and that its callsites appear inside `applyLedger` windows before sizing a hypothesis.

15. **Workload-Specific Entry Types Block Bridge Optimizations**: Several Rust bridge entry points (`contract_code_memory_size_for_rent` for `CONTRACT_CODE`, HashX/pre-auth/signed-payload signer checks, advanced crypto host syscalls) are never triggered by the soroswap benchmark workload. Before proposing optimizations for entry-type-specific or signer-type-specific code paths, confirm the target type appears in the actual workload's ledger-entry mix or account signer vectors.
